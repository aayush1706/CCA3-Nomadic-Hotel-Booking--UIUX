# Nomadic: Hotel Booking UI/UX Redesign
## Usability Testing — Conclusion Report

### 1. Introduction and Objective

The objective of this usability study was to compare the original Booking.com website with Nomadic, a redesigned hotel-booking prototype created in Figma. Five participant transcripts were reviewed to identify usability issues, user preferences, and opportunities for improvement.

All participants were given the same task: find a hotel in London for two adults for three nights, from 12–15 November 2026, compare hotel options, review property details, select a room, and proceed towards payment. The evaluation focused on visual clarity, price transparency, navigation, booking steps, and user confidence.

### 2. Key Findings

**Visual clarity and navigation:** Participants described Booking.com as functional but visually crowded, with advertisements, promotional banners, sign-in prompts, urgency messages, and additional offers competing for attention. Some participants also mentioned extra scrolling and properties opening in new tabs. In contrast, Nomadic’s minimal layout, whitespace, simpler hotel cards, and clearer visual hierarchy were repeatedly appreciated.

**Price transparency:** Price clarity was a major theme in the transcripts. Participants reported needing to double-check taxes, fees, and room-rate conditions on Booking.com. Nomadic’s upfront total-price display, taxes-and-fees messaging, and clearer room-cost breakdowns were viewed positively, particularly by the budget-conscious participant.

**Booking flow and form simplicity:** Participants described Booking.com’s booking process as involving additional offers, pop-ups, and multiple details to review. Nomadic’s three-step flow—room selection, guest details, and payment—was considered easier to follow. The concise guest-details form and visible progress indicators helped communicate what to do next.

**Trust and user confidence:** Scarcity messages, loyalty prompts, and requests for card details raised concerns or caused stress for some participants on Booking.com. Nomadic’s “No account needed” and “Pay at property” messaging was described as reassuring. Participants also appreciated clearly presented room information, including amenities, bed type, cancellation terms, and payment details where displayed.

### 3. Overall Conclusion

Based on the five supplied transcripts, Nomadic was consistently described as a cleaner, calmer, and more straightforward booking experience than the original Booking.com interface. Its main strengths were transparent pricing, reduced visual clutter, clear room information, and a predictable booking journey.

The findings suggest that simplifying the interface and presenting important information at the right stage can help users navigate the booking process with greater confidence. However, the feedback is qualitative and does not establish measured improvements in completion time, error rate, or overall satisfaction.

### 4. Recommendations for Improvement

- Maintain clear upfront pricing, including taxes and fees, throughout the booking journey.
- Keep room details, cancellation conditions, and payment requirements easy to find before users proceed.
- Ensure that buttons, back navigation, form validation, and payment options work consistently in the prototype.
- Continue usability testing with additional participants to identify overlooked issues and validate the design decisions.
- Clearly communicate when payment or booking features are demonstrations rather than live transactions.

### 5. Limitations

This report is based on the five supplied participant transcripts. The UX survey results were not available at the time of writing, so no survey averages, percentages, ratings, or other quantitative findings have been included. The conclusions reflect the feedback contained in the transcripts and should not be treated as statistically representative of all users.

### Final Note

The qualitative findings indicate that Nomadic’s emphasis on simplicity, transparent pricing, and clear navigation was well received by the participants represented in the supplied transcripts. Survey data can be incorporated later to complement these observations with quantitative evidence.
