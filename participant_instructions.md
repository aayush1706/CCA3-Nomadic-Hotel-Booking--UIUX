# Nomadic UI/UX Usability Testing

## 1. Introduction and Instructions

Thank you for participating in this usability-testing study for a college UI/UX design assignment.

You will test two hotel-booking interfaces:
- The original Booking.com website.
- Nomadic, a redesigned hotel-booking prototype created in Figma.

The purpose is to evaluate the usability of the interfaces, not to test your skills. There are no right or wrong answers.

As you use each interface, please **think aloud** by describing what you are looking at, what you expect to happen, what you find confusing, and what you like or dislike.

Please try to complete the same task on both interfaces. You may ask questions, but the moderator will avoid guiding you through the interface so that your natural experience can be observed.

With your permission, the session will be audio-recorded for academic analysis. You should not enter real personal or payment information, and you do not need to complete an actual booking.

Participation and recording are voluntary. Please confirm that you agree to participate and be recorded before the session begins.

## 2. Usability Testing Task

### Scenario

Imagine that you are planning a **three-night trip to London for two adults**.

Your task is to:

1. Find hotels available for your trip.
2. Compare the available hotel options.
3. Open a hotel and review its details and facilities.
4. Select a suitable room, if the interface allows it.
5. Continue to the guest-details stage.
6. Proceed towards payment, without entering real personal or payment information.

### Instructions

- Complete this task first on the original Booking.com website and then on the Nomadic prototype.
- Think aloud throughout the process. Describe your thoughts, expectations, decisions and any difficulties you encounter.
- Use the interface naturally, as you would if planning a real trip.
- If you cannot find an option or proceed to the next step, explain what you expected to happen.
- You do not need to make a real reservation or provide sensitive information.

There is no time limit, and the goal is to understand your experience with both interfaces.
