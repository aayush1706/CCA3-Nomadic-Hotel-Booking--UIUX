Survey Results — Booking.com vs Nomadic
Overview

This document summarizes the usability evaluation of the original Booking.com website and the redesigned Nomadic hotel-booking prototype.

Participants evaluated the interfaces using a 1–10 rating scale, where 1 represents very poor usability and 10 represents outstanding usability.

Survey data status: Complete survey responses were not available when this document was prepared. Therefore, no overall averages or participant-level scores are reported as verified survey findings.

Qualitative Comparison from the Transcripts

The feedback from the five participants indicated that the original Booking.com website had several usability challenges. Participants found the interface visually cluttered because of advertisements, promotional banners, pop-ups, urgency messages, and additional offers. Some participants also felt that the repeated sign-in prompts and upselling interrupted the booking process. Pricing information, particularly taxes and different rate plans, sometimes required extra attention, making it harder to understand the total cost quickly.

In comparison, the Nomadic prototype was generally perceived as cleaner, simpler, and easier to navigate. Participants appreciated the minimal layout, clear visual hierarchy, and limited number of hotel options. The hotel cards presented essential information more directly, allowing users to compare properties without excessive scrolling or distractions. The structured booking process, breadcrumbs, and clearly defined steps also helped participants understand their progress through the booking journey.

Price transparency was another important difference identified in the feedback. Participants appreciated seeing the total price, including taxes, more clearly in Nomadic. Information such as the amount due at the time of booking and the option to pay at the property helped make the process feel more straightforward. Participants also valued the availability of relevant hotel details, including room dimensions and cancellation information, which supported decision-making.

Overall, the transcripts suggest that Nomadic provided a calmer and more focused booking experience than the original Booking.com interface. The main strengths identified were reduced visual clutter, clearer pricing, simplified navigation, and a more structured booking flow. However, further testing could help identify improvements related to payment information, cancellation policies, and interactive functionality. These findings are based on qualitative feedback from five participants and should not be interpreted as statistically representative results.

5. Conclusion

The supplied qualitative feedback suggests that Nomadic's minimalist interface, transparent pricing, and clearly structured booking flow were well received. Participants described the original Booking.com interface as more visually crowded and reported distractions from promotional elements and additional offers.

However, a reliable numerical comparison requires the actual survey responses for both interfaces. Once the data becomes available, criterion-wise averages and a comparative chart can be added to this document.

No missing ratings have been estimated or fabricated.
